Why I created Nexdose

Nexdose was made by a peptide user, for peptide users. I inject several peptides and blends, each on its own schedule. Keeping it all straight was the hard part: which shot today, how much, what time, where to inject next, and when a cycle starts and stops.

I built Nexdose for myself, then for family and friends with the same frustrations. Which means I built it for you too.

What I wanted it to do

  • Answer "what's today?" at a glance. I got tired of notes and spreadsheets, so I stopped logging altogether. Now each shot is scheduled like a calendar event, and logging is one tap. Set it and forget it.
  • Remind me. Life gets busy. Turn on reminders and Nexdose tells you when the next dose is due, at the time you choose.
  • Tell me where to inject next. Pick your body zones, and Nexdose rotates through them so you're not using the same spot over and over. There's a simple map for a few shots and a detailed one for many.
  • Handle cycles for me. Many people cycle on and off, like 5 days on and 2 off, or 8 weeks on and 4 off. Tell Nexdose once and never think about it again.

Why I deleted other apps before I even got started

"Create an account." I know why apps want this: to market to you. I get it, it's tempting as a developer. But I don't want to hand over my personal info before I even know if I like the app. Nexdose has no account, and your data stays on your phone.

"Tell us about yourself." Your goals, your health issues, your weight. I'm not looking for a coach. I know what I'm doing, and I just want a tracker. Nexdose asks no personal questions. Just open it and go.

Too complex. Graphs, tables and logs everywhere made the basics hard to find. Nexdose is built around a calendar, because that's how you live your life. Not a table, not a list. A calendar.

The basics cost money. The features I actually needed were behind a paywall. Nexdose's free version has everything you need out of the box. Plus adds extras for a one-time purchase, never a subscription.
